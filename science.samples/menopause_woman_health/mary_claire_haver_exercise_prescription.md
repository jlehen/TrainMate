# The Menopause Exercise Prescription
*Extracted from Dr. Mary Claire Haver, "The Exercise Prescription I Give Every Woman in Menopause"*

## The Headline Numbers

| Component | Weekly Dose | Daily Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate cardiovascular activity | ~150 minutes | ~20 minutes |
| Resistance (strength) training | ~100 minutes | ~15 minutes |
| **Total** | **~250 minutes** | **~35 minutes** |

**Core principle:** This is a *floor, not a finish line.* If you already train more, keep going. If you're starting from zero, start here — or lower. "The worst dose is zero."

---

## Component 1: Cardio — 150 min/week
- Intensity: **moderate**
- Basis: U.S. Physical Activity Guidelines + WHO guidance; tied to consistent reductions in cardiovascular disease risk.
- Important nuance: 150 is a **public-health target, not a biological cliff.** The dose-response curve is curvilinear — the biggest gains come from going from *nothing* to *something.* Meaningful benefit a
ppears well below 150 minutes.
- **If 150 feels out of reach, start lower.**

## Component 2: Strength — 100 min/week

- Sessions: **2–4 per week**
- Format: **a handful of compound movements**
- Rep range: **8–12 reps per set**
- Equipment/venue: **no gym membership or perfect program required**
- Evidence for the dose: A 2026 analysis of ~147,000 people (majority women, avg. entry age 54) found **90–119 min/week** was the survival sweet spot:
  - 13% lower all-cause mortality
  - 19% lower cardiovascular mortality
  - 27% lower neurological-disease mortality
  - **No additional benefit above 120 min/week**
- Strength work is what "packs the punch" for all-cause mortality and blood pressure, plus strength, physical function, and healthy-aging outcomes that mortality data alone misses.

---

## Sample Weekly Structure

**Option A — Daily 35-minute block (7 days)**
- 20 min moderate cardio + 15 min strength, every day.

**Option B — Split schedule (more practical for most)**

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Strength ~35 min (full body, compound, 8–12 reps) |
| Tue | Cardio ~40 min moderate |
| Wed | Strength ~35 min |
| Thu | Cardio ~40 min moderate |
| Fri | Strength ~35 min (3rd session ≈ 100 min total) |
| Sat | Cardio ~40 min moderate |
| Sun | Cardio ~30 min moderate / active recovery |

*Both options land at ~150 cardio + ~100 strength.*

---

## Non-Negotiable: Do Both

- A network meta-analysis of 81 RCTs found **combined aerobic + resistance training** was the single most effective approach for cardiometabolic health — beating endurance, interval, or resistance training
 alone.
- In the 2026 cohort, **lowest mortality belonged to people doing both at high levels**, not those who picked one.
- "Cardio and strength are not competing options."

## Why Women Especially Should Lift

- Across large cohorts, women get **equal or greater benefit at the same dose** as men — sometimes at roughly **half the weekly volume** for similar survival benefit.
- Weight-training mortality benefit in older adults appears **larger in women than men.**
- In a large Korean cohort, resistance training was linked to lower hypertension risk in women (but not men), with **3–4 sessions/week ≈ 14% risk reduction.**
- Women often show **greater relative** strength gains; men greater absolute gains.

---

## Caveats Stated in the Article

- The 2026 study is **observational and self-reported**, with no data on training intensity — association, not proof.
- **More is not automatically better for everything:** cancer mortality in that analysis was lowest at very *low* strength volumes (1–59 min/week).
- Sex-difference evidence is strongest for *total physical activity and mortality*, not resistance training in isolation. Both sexes benefit substantially.
- **Consistency beats optimization.**
- Educational content only — talk to your own clinician before starting, especially with existing cardiovascular conditions.
