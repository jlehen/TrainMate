## 1. Core Philosophy: Health, Performance, & Durability

Training plans often fail when external life demands shift. A sustainable training plan acts as a flexible home base—a simple, effective foundation focused on long-term compounding adaptations, tissue durability, and health, rather than short-term peak performance. 

* **The Spectrum:** Performance and health overlap significantly, but optimizing fully for one can de-optimize the other. 
* **Prioritize Durability:** Durability, metabolic health, and fatigue resistance allow you to absorb load consistently over years.
* **Individual Application:** Evidence guides the foundation, but individual response determines effectiveness. What works for group means in studies must be tailored to your context.

### Weeks 1–2 — SIT block

#### Weekly Focus

- 0–2/week SIT
- 1/week Resistance Training
- Fill with low-intensity aerobic volume as permitted by time availability and capacity to recover and absorb the training load.
- At least one low-intensity/rest day before each SIT workout.
- If unwilling to do resistance training, should be replaced with another mobility/cross-training activity OR low-intensity. NOT an additional high intensity workout.
- No taper week necessary, but take some de-load days if required before the next training block.

#### Workout Focus — SIT: Sprint Interval Training

- **Work**: 4x ALL-OUT (20–30 sec) sprints/attacks/efforts.
- **Rest**: 5+ min aerobic recoveries.
- **Intensity**: 10/10. ALL-OUT means Priority 1: go as hard as you can (safely). Priority 2: hold it as long as you can (safely).
- **Progression**: No prescription necessary. Progress will come naturally from higher power, better fatigue resistance, and/or longer duration.
- **Purpose**: Builds peripheral working muscle strength and metabolic capacity in very short time, but at the cost of fatigue-resistance.

### Weeks 3–5 — HIIT block

#### Weekly Focus (weeks 3–4)

- 0–2/week HIIT
- 1/week Resistance Training
- Fill with low-intensity aerobic volume as permitted by time availability and capacity to recover and absorb the training load.
- At least one low-intensity/rest day before each HIIT workout.
- If unwilling to do resistance training, should be replaced with another mobility/cross-training activity OR low-intensity. NOT an additional high intensity workout.

#### Weekly Focus (week 5)

**Taper week**: Reduce high intensity to 0–1/week max. 1/week Resistance Training

- Reduce volume of low intensity to ~75% of previous week. Take an extra rest day.

#### Workout Focus — HIIT: High Intensity Interval Training

- **Work**: 15–25 minutes total intensity time (eg. 4x 5-min).
- **Rest**: 2:1 work:rest ratio (eg. 4-min on, 2-min off), aerobic recoveries.
- **Intensity**: 8–9/10. Should be HARD, but sustainable: feel like you could do one more if you had to.
- **Progression**: 4x 4-min → 4x 5-min → 3x 6-min → 4x 6-min → 3x 8-min, etc.
- Prioritize interval duration over intensity: go longer rather than harder.
- **Purpose**: Builds aerobic capacity (eg. VO2max, FTP). Most effective workout type to build overall fitness & performance.

**Taper week**: Reduce high intensity to 0–1/week max. 1/week Resistance Training

- Reduce volume of low intensity to ~75% of previous week. Take an extra rest day.

#### Workout Focus — Threshold: Maximal sustainable aerobic power

- **Work**: 40+ minutes total intensity time (eg. 2x 20-min).
- **Rest**: ~10:1 work:rest ratio, aerobic recoveries (eg. 10-min work, 1-min rest).
- **Intensity**: 6–7/10. Should feel SUSTAINABLE: eg. feel like you could do the entire workout without recovery intervals.
- Prioritize interval duration over intensity: go longer rather than harder.
- **Progression**: 4x 10-min → 3x 15-min → 2x 20-min → 4x 15-min → 3x 20-min, etc.
- **Purpose**: Builds fatigue resistance.

### ... if leading into a priority race

#### Week A-1 — Peak

**Weekly Focus**

- Reduce overall volume to ~75%. More or less based on how you respond to fatigue/freshness (you'll have to experiment to find this balance).
- 0–2/week high-intensity sessions. Make these sessions specific to the event, and make them very high quality, high intensity, but 50–75% of the normal volume of intensity.
- Take an extra rest/active recovery day.
- No strength training necessary.

#### Week A-0 — RACE week

**Weekly Focus**

- Reduce overall volume to ~50%. More or less based on how you respond to fatigue/freshness (you'll have to experiment to find this balance).
- Performing 'openers' leading up to your event can be a good way to keep 'sharpness' while avoiding adding fatigue. Depends on when you want to perform your last high-intensity workout before your event, and how you balance fatigue/freshness.

#### Workout Focus (weeks A-1 and A-0) — High-intensity, race-specific workout

- **Work**: 50–75% of regular volume. Stop when the interval just about starts to feel hard.
- **Rest**: Long, eg. double normal.
- **Intensity**: Same or higher than normal.
- **Prioritize**: Preserve freshness, less is more, hedge toward feeling fresher at the end of the opener workout. Try not to 'test the legs' or 'get one last training session' in.
- **Purpose**: Reduce fatigue, maintain peak 'sharpness'.

## 3. Modular Periodization & Block Sequencing

Rather than a rigid linear calendar, this program is **modular**. The four main blocks (**Sprint Interval Training [SIT]**, **High-Intensity Interval Training [HIIT]**, **Threshold**, and **Low-Intensity Aerobic**) can be cycled in whatever order best fits your goals, season, or equipment.

```
  ↻ SIT  ➔  HIIT  ➔  Threshold  ➔  Aerobic Base ↺
```

### Event-Specific vs. Sustainable Cycling
* **Event Prep (General → Specific):** 
  * *Road:* Aerobic Base → SIT → Threshold → HIIT.
  * *Time Trial / Triathlon:* SIT → HIIT → Threshold.
* **Long-Term Sustainability (Reverse Order):** Starting winter/indoor training with **SIT → HIIT → Threshold** has key benefits:
  1. **Psychological & Logistic:** Short SIT efforts build winter indoor motivation, deliver fast power gains, and avoid high-volume turbo trainer burnout.
  2. **Effort Calibration:** Practicing clear high-intensity efforts in HIIT first teaches you how *severe* intensity feels. When you transition to sub-threshold work later, you are less likely to accidentally overshoot into the severe "grey zone."
* **Integrating Software Targets:** Specialized tools (e.g., WKO5 optimized intervals) can directly replace generic HIIT/Threshold durations. However, SIT should remain strictly all-out and unpaced rather than driven by power targets.

### Ordering the blocks toward a target event

The two Event Prep orderings above are not a lookup table — they are the same rule applied to two different events. Use the rule directly when the athlete's event is not one of those two.

1. **Order by increasing similarity to the event.** Blocks run general → specific, so the block whose demands most resemble the event sits **last, immediately before the taper**. This is why the Road ordering ends on HIIT (road racing is repeated severe surges) while the Time Trial ordering ends on Threshold (a TT is sustained work at maximum steady state). Identify what the event actually demands, then place that block last.

2. **Check the residuals before trusting the order.** An adaptation persists for a while after you stop training it, and the durations differ sharply: aerobic base decays over roughly a month, maximal power in little over a week. So a long aerobic block early in the plan is still working on race day, but a sprint block placed early is gone by then. If the event needs a quality with a short residual, that quality must be trained late or revisited — do not assume one early block covers it. The residual figures, and the physiology behind them, are in the built-in guidelines (`periodization.txt` §3).

3. **Finish with the peak and race weeks.** Whatever the ordering, the last two weeks are the A-1 Peak and A-0 RACE weeks specified in §2.

4. **Fit the plan to the time available.** If there is more time than blocks, prepend low-intensity aerobic volume rather than starting the intensity earlier. If there is less, drop the *earliest* blocks and keep the event-specific one last — never compress by dropping the specific block or by shortening the taper.

5. **No target event, no ordering problem.** This is the plan's default case: cycle the blocks modularly, in whatever order suits the season, equipment, and motivation. The Long-Term Sustainability ordering above is the recommended starting point.

## 4. Low-Intensity Aerobic Training (The Foundation)

Low-intensity volume is the foundation of endurance performance and metabolic health.

* **Dosage:** Perform **0 to 2 structured high-intensity sessions per week at most**. Fill all remaining training time with low-intensity continuous activity.
* **Consistency over Duration:** Shorter, frequent sessions (e.g., 30–45 minutes) still provide valuable durational stimuli and lifestyle energy flux.
* **Volume vs. Intensity:** High intensity cannot replace missing aerobic volume. Volume drives efficiency and durational capacity; high intensity drives capacity. Both require unique stimuli.

---

## 5. Warm-Up Protocol

* **Start Easy:** Begin at a very light resistance/load and gradually increase cadence and power.
* **Hyperemic Sign:** Continue warming up until the first drops of sweat appear—an anecdotal indicator of systemic vasodilation and increased muscle oxygenation.
* **Dynamic Check-In:** Use the warm-up to gauge readiness. If energy or form is poor, reduce the session's physiological stress or reschedule the hard effort.

---

## 6. Sprint Interval Training (SIT)

SIT uses maximal, all-out efforts to target peripheral muscle adaptations (mitochondrial respiration, capillarization, neuromuscular coordination) and preserve fast-twitch motor units lost with age.

* **Prescription:** 2–6 repetitions of **10 to 40 seconds, ALL-OUT** maximal effort with long, complete recoveries (e.g., a sprint every 20–30 minutes during an endurance ride).
* **Execution:** Prioritize 110% safety over pure output. Indoors, use seated accelerations in a big gear to protect equipment; outdoors, use flat, clear roads.
* **Role:** High psychological yield and rapid initial power improvements make SIT an ideal block to kickstart a training cycle.

---

## 7. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)

HIIT is the classic engine-builder (VO2max domain), designed for severe efforts above maximum metabolic steady state (FTP/CP).

* **Prescription:** Bouts **>4 minutes** (e.g., 4x5 min up to 3x8 min) accumulating **15–25 minutes** of total work time.
* **Work/Rest Ratio:** Aim for roughly **2:1 work-to-rest** (e.g., 5 min work / 2.5 min recovery).
* **Pacing & Science:** Meta-analyses show that for time-trial performance, **extending interval duration** and maintaining work consistency yields greater performance returns than pushing extreme intensity or maximizing acute VO2 peak. 
  * Pace efforts at an **RPE of ~8/10**—hard and controlled, leaving "half a rep in the tank."
* **Intermittent HIIT (30/15s):** Alternating 30s work with 15s rest disassociates oxygen flux from mechanical work, keeping VO2 high while allowing partial phosphocreatine (PCr) recovery. This is a valuable novel alternative to sustained bouts.
* **Masters Athletes (50+):** Cap total high-intensity work at **~15 minutes per session** to balance cardiac and systemic recovery, while shifting more focus to strength training and low-intensity volume.

---

## 8. Threshold Training

Threshold workouts build fatigue resistance and durability near your maximum metabolic steady-state (FTP/CP).

* **Prescription:** Long continuous bouts (10+ minutes) aiming to accumulate **40+ minutes** of total time in zone.
* **Work/Rest Ratio:** Use a **~10:1 work-to-rest ratio** (e.g., brief 1-minute tension-release breaks).
* **Pacing Strategy:** **Hedge low by ~10%** rather than overshooting by 2%. Exceeding threshold shifts your physiology into the non-steady-state severe domain, completely changing the fatigue profile and compromising workout volume.

---

## 9. Resistance & Strength Training

Strength training builds structural durability, joint resiliency, and mechanical efficiency. It prevents non-physiological bottlenecks from limiting your load capacity as you age.

* **Prescription:** **1–2 times per week, year-round**. Lift heavy (near technical maximum) with good form.
* **Year-Round Consistency:** Maintaining gym work through the competitive season prevents the severe DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness) that occurs when restarting strength training after a layoff.

---

## 10. Adapting the Plan for Running / Multisport

When adapting this framework to running, modify the programming to respect mechanical impact and eccentric muscle stress:

* **SIT:** Replace all-out maximal sprints with controlled, submaximal **strides and hill accelerations** to avoid acute muscular strain.
* **HIIT & Threshold:** Use slightly longer recovery periods to clear mechanical fatigue, and ramp total volume slower than in cycling to prevent overuse injuries.
