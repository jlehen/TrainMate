# Customizing Ready-Made Training Plans

> **AUTHORITY: REFERENCE ONLY.** This document exists to explain *why* the plan
> works, in athlete-facing rationale and commentary. It is NOT a source of
> workout parameters. Do not derive durations, intensities, block lengths,
> taper percentages, session counts, or block ordering from this document.

This guide synthesizes the source below on customizing a training plan around real life (secondary races, travel, schedule shifts).

**Source:**
- [Easy Ways to Customize Your Readymade Endurance Training Plan](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/customize-your-training-plan/) (TrainingPeaks)

---

Ready-made training plans provide a structured framework, but they often require customization to fit an individual's real-life schedule and race goals.

```
Ready-Made Plan ---> [ Custom Adjustment Process ]
                     |---> Dynamic Scheduling: Hard/Easy Balance Rules
                     |---> Length Manipulation: Shave early weeks or prepend base
                     |---> "B" Race Integration: Introduce partial recovery weeks
                     |---> Interrupted Blocks: Bookend missed travel weeks
```

## 1. Adjusting the Training Schedule (The Shift Protocol)
To shift workouts within your week, follow two key rules:
*   **Rule 1: The Hard/Easy Boundary:** Never schedule key demanding workouts on consecutive days. Ensure at least one easy day (active recovery or rest) separates long rides, high-intensity intervals, or heavy strength sessions.
*   **Rule 2: Balanced Training Distribution:** Space your disciplines evenly throughout the week. For example, a triathlete should avoid placing three runs on consecutive days; instead, alternate run, swim, and bike sessions.

## 2. Adjusting Plan Length
*   **If the Plan is Too Long (Time Gap Prior to Week 1):** Do not start immediately with advanced intensity. Instead, use those extra weeks to prepare your aerobic base, refine your technique, or complete a preparatory strength phase.
*   **If the Plan is Too Short (Time Crunch):** Drop the earliest weeks of the plan, starting at the week that matches your current training volume. If you must skip ahead to a week with higher-intensity intervals, adapt safety margins: replace VO2 max sessions with lighter fartlek sessions, and swap threshold blocks for shorter cruise intervals.

## 3. Integrating "B" Races
"B" races are secondary events used for experience, training, or fun without a full taper.
*   **During a Scheduled Taper/Rest Week:** The "B" race can replace the weekend's scheduled workouts. Lighten your training for 2 days before the race, and allow 3 days of easy recovery afterward.
*   **During a Loading Week:** Do not force a deep taper, as this will disrupt your training momentum. Instead, use a **partial-recovery week** (reducing your training volume by 20% in the days leading up to the race) or a **half-recovery week** (keeping the first half of the week normal and lightening the second half of the week for the race).

## 4. Managing Planned Travel or Missed Training
If you have a holiday or business trip where training will be limited, adjust the surrounding weeks to compensate:

```
        ========================== TRIP BOOKENDING PROTOCOL ==========================
        [ Week 1: Overload Week ] ---> Increase volume/intensity by 10-15% (bike focus)
        [ Week 2: Travel Week   ] ---> Planned travel (e.g. running only, no gym/bike)
        [ Week 3: Transition    ] ---> Gradually step volume back up to baseline
        ==============================================================================
```

By "bookending" the travel, you prepare for the time off with a temporary training overload, using the trip as a functional recovery period. This prevents fitness loss and helps you return to your routine afterward.
