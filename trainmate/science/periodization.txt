# TrainMate Engine Architecture: Periodization & Training Structure Guide

## 1. Core Architectural Overview

The TrainMate engine schedules training time using a hierarchical, nested
structure. Time containers (Macrocycles, Mesocycles, Microcycles) act as
programmatic wrappers, while Periodization Styles (Traditional vs. Block)
dictate the mathematical distribution of training volume and intensity inside
those containers.

*   **Macrocycle** (Overarching Goal Timeline)
    *   **Mesocycle Blocks** (Targeted Phase Adaptation)
        *   **Microcycles** (Fluid Work/Rest Schedule)
            *   **Daily Workout Blueprints**

---

## 2. Component Hierarchy & Container Logic

### A. Macrocycle (The Goal Timeline)

The macrocycle encompasses the entire overarching timeline leading up to an
athlete's primary objective or target event.
* **Duration Constraints:** Programmatic minimum of 5 weeks; maximum of 24
  weeks.
* **System Scheduling Rule:** The engine tracks the *next chronological target
  event* and builds the macrocycle backward from the event date. Timelines
  exceeding 24 weeks must be split into multiple sequential macrocycles
  separated by a formal recovery period.

### B. Mesocycle (The Phase Block)

A medium-sized container dedicated to forcing a specific physical or metabolic
adaptation.
* **Traditional Duration:** 4 to 8 weeks per mesocycle. Changes occur on a
  gradual, shallow gradient.
* **Block Duration:** 2 to 4 weeks per mesocycle. Changes occur on a steep,
  abrupt vertical step function.

#### Mesocycle Loading Progressions:

* **Standard 3:1 Loading (Low-Stress Archetype):** For users under 40 with low
  non-training life stress. 3 weeks of progressive load scaling, followed by a
  1-week deload.
* **Modified 2:1 Loading (High-Stress/Masters Archetype):** triggered if the
  user profile indicates an age of 40+, high self-reported lifestyle stress, or
  complex concurrent training goals. 2 weeks of progressive load scaling,
  followed by a 1-week deload to prevent non-functional overreaching.
* **Deload Protocol:** During a deload week, the system cuts total training
  volume by 30–40% while maintaining target movement intensity at 80–90% of the
  prior week's peak to maintain neural pathways while clearing systemic
  fatigue.

### C. Microcycle (The Fluid Schedule)

The structural unit of daily workout allocation. While default configurations
align to a standard 7-day calendar, the engine must support fluid 10-to-14-day
microcycles for advanced or hybrid athletes to avoid overlapping fatigue.

#### Microcycle Programming Constraints:
* **The Fatigue Buffer:** High-intensity sessions (VO2max intervals, threshold
  workouts, anaerobic sprints) must never be scheduled on consecutive days.
* **Endurance Anchor:** Every microcycle requires exactly one long,
  low-intensity endurance session (typically assigned to weekends or rest
  windows).
* **Rest Mandate:** The engine must enforce 1 to 2 complete rest or active
  recovery days within any rolling 7-day calendar equivalent.

#### Adaptive Stress Consolidation Rule:

For hybrid athletes executing concurrent strength and endurance programming,
the engine must protect designated rest days from hidden central nervous system
(CNS) fatigue. It resolves the conflict between metabolic and neuromuscular
stress by executing a **Tiered Availability Routing Hierarchy**:

* Tier 1: Optimal Same-Day Consolidation (Default / High Availability)

  * **Trigger:** Athlete profile allows for multi-session training days.
  * **Logic:** Consolidate high-intensity endurance elements and heavy strength
    training on the **same calendar day**. 
  * **System Constraints:** Enforce a minimum 6-hour passive recovery buffer
    between sessions. Route the priority adaptation first (e.g., endurance
    first for aerobic profiling; strength first for neuromuscular/hypertrophy
    profiling).

* Tier 2: Alternating Sequential Loading (Standard Availability / 1x Day Cap)

  * **Trigger:** Athlete is logistically restricted to exactly one training
    session per calendar day.
  * **Logic:** The engine concentrates systemic stress over a **48-hour block**
    rather than a 24-hour day. It pairs the heavy strength session and the
    high-intensity endurance session on consecutive days.
  * **System Constraints:** The engine must automatically lock the subsequent
    24-to-48-hour calendar window following this block, forcing either a
    complete rest day or a strict, low-intensity Zone 1/2 recovery session.

* Tier 3: Adaptive System Downgrade (Critical Time / Facility Restrictions)

  * **Trigger:** Athlete has restricted availability, and a compressed
    microcycle layout forces a scheduling collision that violates the 48-hour
    recovery buffer.
  * **Logic:** The engine is strictly forbidden from simply stacking standard
    high-intensity sessions back-to-back without recovery. It must dynamically
    execute one of two fallback algorithms:

    * **The Combined Hybrid Session:** Merge both disciplines into a single
      workout block capping out at a maximum of 75 minutes. Automatically
      truncate accessory strength volume by 50% and restrict endurance to a
      highly condensed interval block.
    * **The Intensity Demotion:** Maintain the primary structural target (e.g.,
      Heavy Strength) as programmed, but automatically downgrade the competing
      session's intensity tier (e.g., converting a VO2max interval workout into
      a flat, steady-state Zone 2 aerobic maintenance ride).

## 3. Algorithmic Periodization Style Selection

The system routes the user into one of two mathematical engines based on
experience level, timeline constraints, and performance goals.

### Engine A: Traditional Linear Model (The Smooth Ramp)

Best suited for beginner-to-intermediate fitness levels, long timelines, and
multi-sport entry-level athletes. 

#### Mathematical Crossover Equation ($TW = V \times I$):

Volume ($V$) and Intensity ($I$) scale gradually in inverse directions across
three distinct, blended mesocycles. Total Workload ($TW$) adjustments are
smooth, changing by a shallow gradient of only $\pm 2\text{--}5\%$
week-over-week.

*   **Phase 1 (Base):** Volume [████████] | Intensity [██]
*   **Phase 2 (Build):** Volume [██████] | Intensity [██████]
*   **Phase 3 (Peak):** Volume [████] | Intensity [████████]

1. **Base Phase (General Physical Preparation - GPP):** 
   * *Duration:* 6 to 12 weeks. 
   * *Math:* Volume is maximized ($80\text{--}100\%$ capacity); intensity is
     restricted ($50\text{--}60\%$ max HR / 1RM). Weekly volume increments grow
     linearly by $+5\text{--}10\%$ during load weeks.
2. **Build Phase (Specific Physical Preparation - SPP):** 
   * *Duration:* 4 to 8 weeks. 
   * *Math:* Volume linearly scales down by $5\text{--}10\%$ weekly; intensity
     linearly scales up by $5\text{--}10\%$ weekly ($75\text{--}85\%$ max
     thresholds). 
   * *Intra-Cycle Variance:* High. Individual microcycles contain high training
     quality diversity, splitting weekly volume across energy systems
     simultaneously (e.g., $75\%$ Aerobic Base, $15\%$ Threshold, $10\%$
     Neuromuscular Speed).
3. **Peak Phase (Competition Prep & Taper):** 
   * *Duration:* 2 to 4 weeks. 
   * *Math:* Volume drops to lowest baseline ($40\text{--}50\%$); intensity
     spikes to maximum ($90\text{--}100\%$ race pace/power).

### Engine B: Block Periodization Model (The Step Function)

Best suited for advanced, highly plateued, or hybrid athletes facing compressed
timelines ($< 10$ weeks).

#### Mathematical Shock Equation:

Volume and intensity do not cross gradually.  Instead, they shift via a steep
step function at mesocycle boundaries. To prevent structural injury under
concentrated loads, individual microcycles maintain extremely low
variance—focusing entirely on a single metabolic or physical adaptation quality
at a time.

*   **Block 1 (Accumulation):** Volume [████████] | Intensity [██]
*   **Block 2 (Transmutation):** Volume [████] | Intensity [████████]
*   **Block 3 (Realization):** Volume [██] | Intensity [████████]

1. **Accumulation Block (Foundational Capacity):**
   * *Duration Boundaries:* Minimum 2 weeks (14 days); Hard Ceiling 6 weeks (42
     days). Recommended default: 3–4 weeks.
   * *Math & Focus:* Massive concentrated training volume; near-zero
     high-intensity intervals. Sharp boundary delta: volume drops by $\sim
     35\%$ overnight upon transition to the next block.
2. **Transmutation Block (High-Intensity Transformation):**
   * *Duration Boundaries:* Minimum 2 weeks (14 days); Hard Ceiling 4 weeks (28
     days). Recommended default: 3 weeks.
   * *Math & Focus:* Intensive, concentrated workloads (VO2max, explosive
     speed, maximal strength). Volume is cut by $50\%$ from baseline; intensity
     immediately steps up to peak thresholds ($+40\%$ delta). 
   * *System Safety Protocol:* The engine triggers a critical error if a
     Transmutation block attempts to schedule a 5th consecutive week,
     automatically forcing an immediate transition to the Realization block to
     avoid systemic autonomic nervous system burnout.
3. **Realization Block
     (The Taper & Peak):**
   * *Duration Boundaries:* Minimum 1 week (7 days); Hard Ceiling 2 weeks (14
     days).
   * *Math & Focus:* Total volume is cratered to eliminate accumulated fatigue,
     while movement intensity remains sharp and crisp to preserve neuromuscular
     recruitment.

## 4. Auto-Regulation & Dynamic Exception Handling

The static calendar structure must dynamically yield to real-time objective and
subjective biological feedback.

* **Physiological Red Flags:** If biometric inputs (e.g., waking HRV suppressed
  below the user's rolling 7-day baseline, or resting heart rate elevated for
  2+ consecutive days) indicate incomplete recovery, the system immediately
  downgrades any scheduled high-intensity sessions to Zone 2 maintenance or
  mandatory rest, regardless of the active mesocycle rules.
* **Business Travel Exception:** For designated heavy travel days, the engine
  caps workout duration to a maximum of 30–45 minutes, down-regulates session
  intensity, or converts the calendar block into an active rest day.
* **Vacation Exception:** Based on travel constraints, the engine dynamically
  recalibrates to maintain base aerobic fitness via non-specific local
  disciplines (e.g., hiking, swimming), or programmatically repurposes the
  vacation block as a scheduled mesocycle deload.
* **Social / Major Lifecycle Events:** The engine blocks high-intensity
  sessions from being scheduled on the calendar day of, or the morning
  immediately following, a major social event to mitigate increased injury
  risks associated with sleep deprivation, fatigue, or dehydration.
