# TrainMate Engine Architecture: Periodization & Training Structure Guide

## 1. Core Architectural Overview

The TrainMate engine schedules training time using a hierarchical, nested
structure. Time containers (Macrocycles, Mesocycles, Microcycles) act as
programmatic wrappers, while Periodization Styles (Traditional vs. Block)
dictate the mathematical distribution of training volume and intensity inside
those containers.

*   **Macrocycle** (Overarching Goal Timeline)
    *   **Mesocycle Blocks** (Targeted Phase Adaptation)
        *   **Microcycles** (Fluid Work/Rest Schedule)
            *   **Daily Workout Blueprints**

---

## 2. Component Hierarchy & Container Logic

### A. Macrocycle (The Goal Timeline)

The macrocycle encompasses the entire overarching timeline leading up to an
athlete's primary objective or target event.
* **Duration Constraints:** Programmatic minimum of 5 weeks; maximum of 24
  weeks.
* **System Scheduling Rule:** The engine tracks the *next chronological target
  event* and builds the macrocycle backward from the event date. Timelines
  exceeding 24 weeks must be split into multiple sequential macrocycles
  separated by a formal recovery period.

### B. Mesocycle (The Phase Block)

A medium-sized container dedicated to forcing a specific physical or metabolic
adaptation.
* **Traditional Duration:** 4 to 8 weeks per mesocycle. Changes occur on a
  gradual, shallow gradient.
* **Block Duration:** 2 to 4 weeks per mesocycle. Changes occur on a steep,
  abrupt vertical step function.

#### Mesocycle Loading Progressions:

* **Standard 3:1 Loading:** 3 weeks of progressive load scaling, followed by a
  1-week deload.
* **Modified 2:1 Loading:** triggered based on the learnings, high
  self-reported lifestyle stress, or on user request.
  2 weeks of progressive load scaling, followed by a 1-week deload to prevent
  non-functional overreaching.
* **Deload Protocol:** During a deload week, the system cuts total training
  volume by 30–40% while maintaining target movement intensity at 80–90% of the
  prior week's peak to maintain neural pathways while clearing systemic
  fatigue.

### C. Microcycle (The Fluid Schedule)

The structural unit of daily workout allocation. While default configurations
align to a standard 7-day calendar, the engine must support fluid 10-to-14-day
microcycles for advanced or hybrid athletes to avoid overlapping fatigue.

#### Microcycle Programming Constraints:
* **The Fatigue Buffer:** High-intensity sessions (VO2max intervals, threshold
  workouts, anaerobic sprints) must never be scheduled on consecutive days.
* **Endurance Anchor:** Every microcycle requires exactly one long,
  low-intensity endurance session (typically assigned to weekends or rest
  windows).
* **Rest Mandate:** The engine must enforce 1 to 2 complete rest or active
  recovery days within any rolling 7-day calendar equivalent.
