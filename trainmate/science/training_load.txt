TRAINING LOAD MONITORING GUIDELINES (ACWR + FITNESS/FATIGUE MODEL)
==================================================================

Training load monitoring answers two related questions:
  (a) Is load being ramped safely, or spiking into injury risk?   -> ACWR
  (b) Where is the athlete on the fitness / fatigue / freshness curve, and how
      should load progress week over week and taper into an event?  -> PMC (CTL/ATL/TSB)

Both models operate on the same underlying daily training load.
   - Load metric (per session): TSS, or (RPE * Duration) when power/pace is
     unavailable. Sum per calendar day to get daily load.


1. ACUTE:CHRONIC WORKLOAD RATIO (ACWR)
--------------------------------------
The ACWR compares short-term load to long-term load to flag load spikes.

   - Acute Workload:   sum of daily load over the past 7 days.
   - Chronic Workload: average weekly load over the past 28 days
                       (total 28-day load / 4).
   - ACWR = Acute Workload / Chronic Workload

   Interpretation:
   - ACWR < 0.8:       Under-training. Fitness low/declining; a sudden spike from
                       here is itself risky.
   - ACWR 0.8 - 1.3:   "Sweet Spot". Load is safely building or maintained.
   - ACWR 1.3 - 1.5:   "Danger Zone". Elevated fatigue; caution advised.
   - ACWR > 1.5:       "Injury Threshold". Acute load has spiked too fast.
                       Reduce load immediately.

   Caveat: ACWR is a useful guardrail, not a precise predictor. Its coupled
   rolling-average form has known methodological weaknesses, so treat it as one
   input, cross-checked against the fitness/fatigue model below and recovery
   metrics (HRV, RHR, sleep).


2. FITNESS / FATIGUE / FORM (PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT CHART, PMC)
--------------------------------------------------------------
The PMC (Banister impulse-response model) tracks three exponentially-weighted
moving averages of daily load. It captures accumulated fitness, short-term
fatigue, and the balance between them ("form").

   - CTL (Chronic Training Load) = "Fitness"
       Exponentially-weighted moving average of daily load, ~42-day time
       constant. Rises slowly; represents accumulated fitness.
   - ATL (Acute Training Load) = "Fatigue"
       Exponentially-weighted moving average of daily load, ~7-day time
       constant. Rises and falls quickly; represents recent fatigue.
   - TSB (Training Stress Balance) = "Form" = CTL(yesterday) - ATL(yesterday)
       Positive = fresh/tapered; negative = fatigued/loaded.

   TSB interpretation (approximate bands):
   - TSB > +25:        Very fresh but detraining risk if sustained (fitness
                       decaying). Only appropriate right before an A event.
   - TSB +5 to +25:    Peaked / race-ready freshness. Target band for an A-event
                       taper.
   - TSB -10 to +5:    Neutral / "grey zone". Maintenance or light build.
   - TSB -10 to -30:   Productive overload. Normal and desirable inside a build
                       block: fitness is being built while fatigue is carried.
   - TSB < -30:        Excessive fatigue. High overreaching / injury risk;
                       insert recovery.

   Relationship to ACWR: ATL vs CTL is conceptually the same acute-vs-chronic
   comparison as ACWR, over exponential (not flat) windows. Use ACWR/ramp-rate
   for injury-risk pacing; use TSB for freshness, peaking, and tapering.


3. WEEK-OVER-WEEK PROGRESSION: CTL RAMP RATE
--------------------------------------------
"How much to increase load week over week" is governed by the CTL ramp rate:
the weekly rise in CTL, expressed in load units per day per week (e.g. TSS/day
per week).

   - Ramp rate ~3-5:   Sustainable for most athletes building fitness.
   - Ramp rate ~5-7:   Aggressive; acceptable for experienced athletes in a
                       focused build block, monitored closely.
   - Ramp rate > 8:    High risk of non-functional overreaching / injury.
                       Avoid outside short, deliberate overload weeks.

   As a volume heuristic, this aligns with limiting weekly volume increases to
   roughly 5-10% (the classic "10% rule" is a soft ceiling, not a validated
   law). Ramp on load, not on a fixed percentage, and back off when recovery
   metrics or TSB indicate the athlete is not absorbing the load.

   ACWR vs ramp rate - complementary, not redundant: ACWR (short flat windows,
   relative) detects an acute one-week spike; ramp rate (42-day EWMA, absolute)
   detects an unsustainable multi-week climb. They often move together but can
   diverge - a single big week can push ACWR > 1.5 while ramp stays fine, and a
   smooth relentless build can hold ACWR near the sweet spot while ramp exceeds
   8. Treat EITHER crossing its red line as a caution; a safe ACWR does not
   clear an aggressive ramp, and vice versa.


4. COACHING DIRECTIVES
----------------------
   - Build CTL gradually via ramp rate; keep ACWR in the 0.8-1.3 sweet spot.
     Check both: a red flag on EITHER (ACWR > 1.5 OR ramp rate > 8) warrants
     caution even when the other looks fine.
   - When ACWR rises above 1.3, favor lower-intensity sessions or trimming
     planned volume to avoid crossing 1.5.
   - When ACWR drops below 0.8, rebuild load gradually; a ramp rate around 5 or
     below (roughly a 10-15% weekly volume increase) is a reasonable ceiling.
   - When TSB falls below -30, default to recovery/deload and override continued
     loading regardless of ACWR - unless this is a short, deliberate overreach
     block, in which case cap its duration and watch recovery metrics closely.
   - To peak for an A event, taper load so TSB rises into the +5 to +25 band by
     event day while preserving intensity (see periodization deload/realization
     protocol).
   - Sustained TSB well above +25 means fitness is decaying: resume building.
   - Cross-check all of the above against recovery metrics (HRV, RHR, sleep);
     depressed recovery overrides a "safe" ACWR/TSB.
